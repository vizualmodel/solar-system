# Apollo 11 · From Earth to the Moon

This is a built-in guided mission, available without the companion. Select it and choose **Start mission**, then **Play mission**. The structured scenario document is `src/data/apollo-11.json`. It describes 16 timed chapters, their historical mission elapsed start/end times, viewing durations, original captions and suggested cameras. The initial presentation lasts 14 minutes 15 seconds at 1×.

Click a chapter to jump there paused. Drag to inspect and scroll to zoom. Pause freezes spacecraft motion and mission time. The chapter progress display replaces the ordinary time scrubber; presentation speed changes how fast the story plays. Stop mission restores the complete previous simulation state, paused. The previous views, camera poses, range and speed remain available throughout the mission and are retained in saves.

This first edition uses captions and a static lunar surface. Historical event times are based on NASA mission documentation and the Apollo Flight Journal; captions are original summaries. The spacecraft trajectories, attitudes, stage separations and local terrain are illustrative reconstructions. Hourly geometric vectors from NASA/JPL Horizons provide Earth–Moon context for 16–25 July 1969. Normal observatory date coverage remains unchanged.

NASA/Michael D. Carbajal Saturn V and Lunar Module assets are used with separate geometric sections for staging. The command/service module is a locally authored exterior based on Apollo proportions. The landed descent stage remains behind during ascent. Optional NASA launch and crew/ground landing recordings are available via Recordings in the playback bar. Speech stays at original speed; these are chapter highlights rather than exact synchronization. Video and astronaut animation remain for later. Split camera windows horizontally or vertically, then choose independent viewpoints. The flight-dashboard button hides or recalls Apollo-inspired instruments with estimated speed, Earth/Moon altitude and mission elapsed time. The launch platform, tower, staged flames and smoke are illustrative.
