# Guided mission scenarios

Mission documents are versioned JSON under `src/data/`, registered in `src/lib/mission.ts`. The companion-prompt Markdown catalogue remains compatible; a mission ID in the scenario picker starts the deterministic browser player rather than submitting a model turn. `scenarios/apollo-11.md` explains the authored mission.

Every chapter defines a unique `id`, `title`, historical mission elapsed `start`/`end` (`HHH:MM:SS`), presentation `duration` in seconds, a flight-adapter `scene`, suggested `camera` and distance in metres, and caption cues with normalized `at` values. Optional `timeMap` anchors distribute viewing time within a historical interval: docking and re-entry can linger over brief events. Optional chapter-start `media` audio cues use local `/audio/` assets, `at: 0`, and required credits. NASA launch and landing communications are included. Recordings are opt-in, pause with the mission, restart on chapter re-entry, and retain original speech speed at every presentation rate. These are archival highlights, not word-for-word synchronization with the accelerated reconstruction. Video remains reserved. Source links and accuracy text belong to the document. Runtime validation rejects inconsistent intervals, caption cues and time maps.

`advanceMission` consumes presentation time and advances automatically, carrying excess time into later chapters. Chapter selection reconstructs state directly and pauses; it never replays earlier side effects. The end pauses on splashdown, with Replay and Stop available. Rates are 0.5×/1×/2×. Guided cameras reset only on chapter entry or an explicit reset; dragging and pausing remain available. Normal timeline UI and normal companion time/session mutations are locked while a mission runs. Stop restores `returnState` with playback paused. Nested missions are rejected. JSON saves retain the mission, manual camera and complete original state; reloading resumes paused. Companion activation captures and pauses both clocks.

The normal simulation clock and views remain suspended in the background. A dedicated mission viewport uses the 1969 dataset, so existing 1990–2030 dates and saved ranges do not change. Observations expose the background `time` plus an explicit `mission.time`, ID, chapter and playback flag while active. Historical vectors use UTC/UT request times and Hermite interpolation of hourly NASA/JPL Horizons position/velocity samples in J2000 ecliptic coordinates. No extrapolation outside the downloaded interval is allowed.

`mission-flight.ts` is the Apollo flight adapter. Transfer arcs, launch/descent profiles, orbit phase, spacecraft attitudes, stage offsets, rendezvous, weather and lunar terrain are educational illustrations. They are not trajectory solutions or flight telemetry. Flight paths cannot be used for navigation. Adding a different mission reuses the player but also requires its own validated flight/vehicle adapter and historical context. ReTLEctor/CelesTrak satellite data are not used for Apollo.

NASA models: [Saturn V](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/saturn-v/) and [Apollo Lunar Module](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/apollo-lunar-module/), NASA/Michael D. Carbajal. The original models are downloaded by `scripts/fetch-apollo-assets.mjs`, together with dedicated JPL context; existing downloads are retained. Models are normalized to 110.6 m rocket height and 7 m LM height. Spatial triangle partitions permit illustrative staging even where authoring meshes span multiple stages. Columbia is a local geometric model with separable command and service modules, radiator panels, engine, RCS quads, windows, docking probe and parachutes. No astronaut animation or video is included. `scripts/fetch-apollo-audio.mjs` downloads the attributed NASA Historical Sounds recordings and writes `public/audio/apollo-11/credits.json`.

Mission windows have independent camera modes (story, follow spacecraft, Earth, Moon, overview, launch pad), nested horizontal/vertical splits and close/reset controls, up to six views. Version-1 single-camera saves remain readable. Window layout, poses, dashboard visibility and audio settings persist in the mission without changing the suspended observatory views. The Apollo-inspired dashboard has a velocity gauge, mission elapsed time and Earth/Moon surface altitudes, estimated from the illustrative trajectory at historical time. Its toggle remains in the playback toolbar. The launch complex is a procedural illustrative platform, umbilical tower and trench; staged plumes and ground smoke reconstruct deterministically on pause/seek.

Trajectory display: the timeline orbit icon (show/hide trajectories) toggles both paths across wide mission views. Gold shows the illustrative spacecraft transfer leg (or one parking orbit); blue shows the Moon centre from launch to splashdown, sampled from the historical JPL vectors. Paths share Earth-centred, non-rotating J2000 ecliptic axes. Camera tracking changes the rendering origin by translation only; Earth surface rotation does not rotate either path. The optional `trajectoriesVisible` preference defaults to visible for older saves and is preserved with the mission.
